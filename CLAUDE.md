# Portfolio site, Kristjan Mihin

Personal portfolio and freelance site. Owner: Kristjan Mihin, full-stack developer (.NET / Azure / React), Slovenia.

## Stack
- Astro (static output), Tailwind CSS, TypeScript.
- Client JS: three, gsap (ScrollTrigger), lenis, loaded via <script> in Astro components. Keep scripts small and commented. No UI framework on the client.
- Design (v5, "velvet, stone, brass"): three materials. Midnight velvet (#121f3a / deep #070c19), warm ivory travertine (#ebe2d1 / light #f5efe3) and brushed brass (#c9a86a / deep #9c7c45) as the one accent (hairlines, numerals, link underlines, the seam star, selection). A fixed full-page WebGL background (`src/scripts/silk.ts`, Three.js shader) renders velvet (long diagonal folds with shadowed valleys, cool sheen, two inks of blue drifting, fine pile, rare thin brass threads, warm lamp glow upper right, vignette) or travertine (mottle, thin veins, pits, bedding bands) and eases between them. Chapters carry `data-theme="midnight" | "ivory"`; `motion.ts` switches `<html data-theme>` when a chapter reaches mid-screen, the material eases to it and the text palette follows via CSS variables (`--ink`, `--muted`, `--line`, `--panel`, `--panel-strong`, `--contrast`, `--accent`). Tailwind tokens map to those (`text-ink`, `text-accent`, `bg-contrast`, `border-line`, `.panel` translucent blur surface, `.panel-strong` solid, `.rule` brass hairline). The page is velvet with one stone chapter (Work); `Seam.astro` (short band: brass four-point star on a hairline, turns with the scroll) marks each change of material. Fonts self-hosted via @fontsource-variable: Bodoni Moda (display, weight 500, optical size held at 36 so hairlines survive 1x screens; italic of the same family for emphasis) + Geist (body). Sharp corners everywhere (no border-radius). Motion: one layer in `src/scripts/motion.ts` (GSAP ScrollTrigger + Lenis), started on `astro:page-load` and torn down on `astro:before-swap` (Astro view transitions fade between pages). Hooks are data attributes: `data-split` (word-by-word headline rise), `data-rule` (hairline draws in), `.reveal` / `.reveal-item` (rise + stagger), `data-enter` (above-the-fold load-in), `.shield` (soft blurred halo behind text that the cloud may drift behind), `data-showcase` (a chapter that cycles through its `[data-item]`s on its own, `src/scripts/showcase.ts`: crossfade with blur, brass fill under the active index entry showing the wait, keeps running under the pointer, pauses off screen / on a hidden tab and waits 8 s after a pick; `data-interval` seconds per item; no cards or frames around items). The header is hidden on the opening screen of the home page (`headerHidden` on Base), appears on the first scroll up, hides on scroll down, and marks the link of the chapter on screen (`aria-current`); in-page links scroll smoothly (the click handler runs in the capture phase so Astro's router does not jump first). The home intro (Intro.astro) owns its own 320vh scroll timeline: masthead name (surname italic) under a brass rule, my photo cut out (`public/portrait-cutout.webp`, background removed) standing on the right behind the name, toned down and fading into the velvet at the bottom, and behind everything the particles form a spiral galaxy (`galaxy()` in particles.ts: bright core behind the head, three arms winding around the photo, disk tipped toward the viewer; `data-faint-until` keeps the cloud at 60% until that point of the intro), then a short statement with a brass italic ending and three pillars, then brass counters over a slowly turning particle globe; they stay to the end of the pin and scroll away together with the globe (the intro stage is the cloud's anchor, `data-cloud-fit="none"`). The intro panels lie on top of each other, so only links and buttons take pointer events. A full-page particle layer (`src/scripts/particles.ts`, Three.js points, fixed above the material) forms whatever scene the section on screen asks for: `[data-shapes]` sections (intro, closing) are turned into scroll keyframes by `motion.ts`, showcase chapters flow the cloud into the scene of the item on show (pairs are written `"a+b"` and shown side by side); when a showcase takes the cloud over from another section, the old shape fades out where it is and the first scene fades in already formed in the chapter's box (`setFade`). The cloud is anchored: a section that owns it (it covers the middle of the screen) points it at its `[data-cloud]` box, the cloud is centred and scaled into that box and follows it exactly, so a scene leaves with its section. When the anchor changes the shape on screen is frozen in the new anchor's frame (`freezeCloud`), so nothing jumps. Drawn shapes put a third of their points on the edges so they read crisp. A motion can start from `"current"`, a frozen copy of whatever the cloud shows, so nothing ever jumps. Tech marks come from the `simple-icons` package (C#, Azure, SQL as lettering because Microsoft marks are not in that set); drawn scenes live in `shapes.ts`; helpers for placing the cloud in `cloud.ts`. `data-side` on the intro says where the text is for its ring. Dots are brass on velvet and midnight on stone. CSS motion tokens in global.css; hover effects gated behind `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion` (`.reduce-motion` html class disables start states and pinning, showcases do not cycle and switch items without movement; the material still renders, static, in the first chapter's colour). Content lives in `content/` as Markdown with frontmatter. Pages are built from it; never hardcode case-study text in components. Hyphenated words in item titles are kept on one line from sm up (`src/lib/words.ts`).
- Copy rules: no em/en dashes in visible text (use commas, colons, periods, or a plain hyphen for ranges); no uppercase "eyebrow" labels above headings; no scroll cues.
- Deploy target: Cloudflare Pages (static). Keep the build host-agnostic.

## Code conventions
- Simple, readable code. No clever abstractions.
- No inline styles. Tailwind utility classes or scoped component CSS.
- Small components, one responsibility each.
- Type all frontmatter with Astro content collections (`src/content.config.ts`).
- English UI. Slovenian translation may come later; keep strings in content, not in markup.

## Content rules
- Professional work (Trinet) is described anonymously: no client names, no screenshots with real data, no internal system names beyond what is in `content/professional/`.
- Side projects may link to GitHub repos.
- AI-assisted scaffolding (Lovable) is disclosed where used.

## Pages
- `/` is the whole site, one scroll: Intro (velvet, pinned 320vh: opening line with the two calls to action and the full-width masthead name over my cut-out photo and the particle galaxy, no header on this screen, then statement with three pillars, facts) -> `#skills` Expertise (velvet) -> brass seam -> `#work` Work (stone; each study shows title, summary and a three-column facts grid) -> brass seam -> `#offer` What I offer (velvet) -> `#contact` closing statement + contact (velvet; the cloud forms an envelope in a sticky box on the left; my photo `public/portrait.jpg` sits beside the contact heading, monochrome with a brass top line). The three chapters are `Showcase.astro`: title and lead, an index of the items (clickable), one item on show with previous / next arrows, the cloud forming the item's scene on the right; the chapter advances on its own (4 s, Work 5 s) while on screen. One contact label on the page: "Get in touch".
- Scenes are named in `src/scripts/shapes.ts` (drawn primitives) and `src/scripts/particles.ts` (tech marks). Skills come from `content/skills.json`, services from `content/services.json` (each with a `shape`), work from the case-study collections (`label` is the short index name) with the scene per slug set in `index.astro`.
- `/work/[slug]` detail pages still exist for the professional case studies (linked as "Full case study" when a study has no live site or repo).
