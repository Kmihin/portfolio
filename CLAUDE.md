# Portfolio site, Kristjan Mihin

Personal portfolio and freelance site. Owner: Kristjan Mihin, full-stack developer (.NET / Azure / React), Slovenia.

## Stack
- Astro (static output), Tailwind CSS, TypeScript.
- Client JS: three, gsap (ScrollTrigger), lenis, loaded via <script> in Astro components. Keep scripts small and commented. No UI framework on the client.
- Design (v12, "board": a light, high-tech site over a 3D circuit board that is my system): one fixed full-screen Three.js canvas behind every page (`src/scripts/board.ts`, `#board` in `Base.astro`). The board: an unlit silkscreen plane in the page colour (#f3f5f9; grid, footprints, part designators, mounting holes, "KM-BOARD REV 2026", fading out at the edges, drawn on a canvas), a `ShadowMaterial` plane for soft shadows (PCF, desktop only), and graphite chips with printed tops (canvas textures from the page content): U1 the CPU in the middle with an aluminium lid engraved "KM-01 / KRISTJAN MIHIN / FULL-STACK DEVELOPER", U2-U8 a chip per technology (`content/skills.json` titles), U11-U17 a module per case study (`label` from the case studies), M1-M4 memory chips with the figures printed on them, J1 an I/O port for contact; pins, tidy rows of resistors and capacitors. Traces (flat ribbons, `route()`: out, a 45 degree run, in) run from the CPU to every part; data pulses (short blue streaks, a few coral) race along them. The labels come from `#board-data` (JSON on the home page; case pages have plain chips). On load the board builds itself: the traces draw out from the CPU (shader `uDraw`), the parts rise from the CPU outwards (easeOutBack), the camera comes down from above. Camera stops (`STOPS` in board.ts: point on the board, distance, elevation, azimuth, `shift` = how far the view is panned so the point sits right of the text, `focus` = which parts the section is about): every section carries `data-scene="name"`; `motion.ts` (`followStops`) takes the section whose top has passed mid-screen and the next one and the camera eases along the way between their stops. The focused parts lift, their tops glow blue, their traces turn blue with a running sheen, and the pulses favour them. The pointer tilts the view a little. Pixel ratio capped (2, phones 1.5), no shadows on phones, nothing drawn while the tab is hidden; reduced motion: the board is built at once and the camera holds the first stop. Over the canvas a white haze (`.haze`) on the left and along the top and bottom (all over on phones), so text sits on calm ground; content sits in the left 62% on wide screens. Pinned scenes (`data-pin="200"` pins for 200% of the viewport, scrubbed; only when the section fits the screen): inside, `[data-out]` fades out over the first half and `[data-in]` fades in over the second (the hero: the name gives way to the statement). Preloader (`#preloader`): a monospace counter 000 to 100 ("Booting the system"; at least 1.4 s and not before the board's first frame), then the curtain lifts and the hero stages in (`data-enter`). Custom cursor (`#cursor`, fine pointers only): a blue dot on the pointer and a ring trailing it that grows and turns blue over links (`.is-hot`); the system cursor is hidden while it runs. Palette (global.css): off-white (#f3f5f9), white surfaces, graphite ink (#0b1220), muted (#5a6577), one electric blue accent (#2f5bff, soft #e6ecff), coral (#ff6a3d) as a signal colour. Fonts: Geist (self-hosted, the only webfont; headings weight 600, tight tracking; `<em>` in a heading is the one word in the accent, upright) and the system monospace stack for labels (`.label`, `.label-mark` adds a small blue square), section numbers with their board reference ("U1 / 01", "J1 / 05"), numbers and tags. Surfaces: `.glass` (white translucent panel, square corners, crop marks in two corners like a technical drawing), `.shade` (a soft light halo behind free text), `.scene` (a full-screen section that can be pinned). Square buttons (`.btn-primary` graphite, blue with a glow on hover; `.btn-secondary` white outlined). Header (`Header.astro`): the name, the chapter counter in the middle ("02 / 08  Work", from `data-chapter-name`; hidden on phones) and "Let's talk"; hides on scroll down and returns on scroll up; in-page links scroll smoothly (capture-phase click handler). Motion hooks: `data-split` (words rise out of a clipped line; keeps an `<em>` child on its words), `data-rule`, `.reveal` / `.reveal-item`, `data-enter`, `data-count` (numbers count up when they enter). `html` has `overflow-x: clip`. Everything honours `prefers-reduced-motion`. Content lives in `content/`; never hardcode case-study text in components.
- Copy rules: no em/en dashes in visible text (use commas, colons, periods, or a plain hyphen for ranges); the monospace status lines, chapter numbers and the scroll cue are intended.
- Deploy target: Cloudflare Pages (static). Keep the build host-agnostic.

## Code conventions
- Simple, readable code. No clever abstractions.
- No inline styles. Tailwind utility classes or scoped component CSS.
- Small components, one responsibility each.
- Type all frontmatter with Astro content collections (`src/content.config.ts`).
- English UI. Slovenian translation may come later; keep strings in content, not in markup.

## Content rules
- The transport / logistics field I work in now is never named (I cannot take projects in that field): the e-CMR work is shown in general terms as `document-platform.md` ("Digital document exchange platform"), the ReBAC work as "Security, roles and rights for a B2B platform"; the carrier import stays hidden (`_carrier-data-import.md`, skipped by the collections). The apartment site is not named ("Holiday apartment website").
- Professional work (Trinet) is described anonymously: no client names, no screenshots with real data, no internal system names beyond what is in `content/professional/`.
- Side projects may link to GitHub repos.
- AI-assisted scaffolding (Lovable) is disclosed where used.

## Pages
- `/` is the whole site, one scroll across the board: `#home` hero (pinned 200%, the whole board building itself on the right; the name gives way to the statement with the buttons; the stack and a scroll cue at the bottom) -> `#about` U1 / 01 About me (pinned when it fits; the camera on the CPU: portrait, heading, three paragraphs, four values) -> `#facts` M1-M4 (pinned 120%; the memory chips with the figures, the same figures in four panels counting up) -> `#work` U11 / 02 Selected work (the case study modules: `ChapterHead.astro` + `WorkList.astro`, rows linking to the case study pages) -> `#skills` U2-U8 / 03 Tech expertise (the technology chips: `Expertise.astro`, panels from `content/skills.json`) -> `#offer` BUS / 04 What I offer (a low view across the whole lit board: `Offer.astro`, a numbered list from `content/services.json`) -> `#closing` (pinned 150%; straight down on the whole board, the statement on a panel) -> `#contact` J1 / 05 Let's talk (the I/O port: email button, links, footer line; the home page passes `footer={false}` to Base).
- Work comes from the case-study collections (featured first, then most recent).
- `/work/[slug]` is a page for every case study (stop detail, the board far behind): label, title, blue tagline and summary, the cover, the feature grid (`CaseFeatures.astro`), a sticky panel with role, stack tags and links beside the markdown body; a "Next" band and `Footer.astro`.
